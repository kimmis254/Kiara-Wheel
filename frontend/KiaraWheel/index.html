Origin of Kiara Wheel

The concept of purpose has existed in many cultures. One particular culture that stands out of this is Japanese Culture. Japanese have a term called Ikigai, which means the 'reason to live.'

The Ikigai concept unites the joy of Life with a sense of Purpose. The term is a combination of the Japanese words 'iki' ('to live') and 'gai' (reason)—thus 'reason to live' or, in other words, your Purpose. For the Japanese, Ikigai is more of a way of Life, a journey, than a destination you target.

In Kenya, from my heritage, the Kikuyu community believed that everyone was born with a specific gift or "Kiara." The word "Kiara" is a Kikuyu word that means "a finger" or more appropriately “magical fingers.”

It was believed that if one had "Kiara," whatever they touched prospered, similar to the "Midas touch"—a Greek myth in which King Midas turned anything he touched to gold. We had people gifted as medicine men, warriors, and hunters. I am grateful to Dad for sharing this important aspect of our culture.

Many cultures had similar views and ways of life. Purpose, then, is not a new concept.
